# Oregon ordinary-injury review — October 5, 2026

The separate `.4` candidate adds a conditional ordinary-negligence bodily-injury calculation: two years from independently confirmed accrual, compared with ten years from the defendant's qualifying act or omission complained of. It is not yet registered or published. The current published release remains `.3`; Oregon remains research-only there until the candidate passes independent review, registration, storage verification and publication.

## What resolved the earlier historical gap

The [official majority opinion in Marshall v. PricewaterhouseCoopers, LLP, 371 Or. 536 (2023)](https://ojd.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/api/collection/p17027coll3/id/10540/download), at 539–40, identifies ORS 12.115 as 1967 c.406 §2 and states that its text had remained unchanged. At 544–46, the majority traces SB 134's amendments and the compromise that created the ten-year provision; page 546 also identifies the ordinary two-year period. This is stronger identification evidence than the earlier caption/text comparison alone. The separate “same bill” wording at page 566 appears in a dissent and is not relied upon as a majority holding.

The [official enrolled SB 134 file](https://records.sos.state.or.us/ORSOSWebDrawer/Record/2893072/File/document), physical pages 17–18, preserves the enacted-text form and its certification cover. Section 2 matches ORS 12.115 and ends without an express effective-date, applicability or savings clause. The chapter, executive approval and filing fields remain blank in this copy; the review does not fill them in. The [official 1967 disposition table](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/lawsstatutes/1967.pdf), printed page 225, independently maps c.406 §§1–2 to ORS 12.110/.115. The contemporaneous 1967 Chapter 12 codification provides additional text corroboration.

The candidate's **September 13, 1967 boundary is derived**, not printed in the bill copy. [Article IV §28 of the Oregon Constitution](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/orcons.html) supplies the post-session restriction; the [Legislative Counsel Drafting Manual](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/lc/PDFs/draftingmanual.pdf), chapter 12, page 12.1 and footnote 1, distinguishes the pre-1999 normal rule from the later January 1 default. The [official 1967 session guide](https://records.sos.state.or.us/ORSOSWebDrawer/Record/6785334/File/document) dates adjournment to June 14. The 91st day is September 13. The captured enrolled text contains no special date provision. The candidate excludes both earlier accrual and earlier complained-of acts, and does not decide retroactivity or prior proceedings.

## Scope and source quality

The [2025 ORS Chapter 12](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/bills_laws/ors/ors012.html) supplies §§12.010, 12.110(1) and 12.115(1)–(2). The [2026 Volume 1 update](https://www.oregonlegislature.gov/lc/ORSupdate/Volume01.pdf) lists no chapter 12 amendment or newly added chapter 12 section. This is bounded currentness evidence, not a certification of all Oregon law or subsequent judicial treatment.

Marshall concerns legal negligence and economic loss. The candidate uses it for enactment history and the distinction between the two clocks; it does not infer an ordinary-injury accrual date from that case. The user must independently confirm accrual, the complained-of act and application of both provisions. Products, professional negligence, government defendants, construction, intentional torts, abuse/assault, latent/toxic exposure, wrongful death, survival, economic loss and unresolved exceptions are excluded. Tolling, borrowing, commencement/service and court calendars remain separate review issues.

The candidate retains downloaded originals and their original receipts. The enrolled bill and Marshall source readers contain expressly labelled selected manual transcriptions of visually reviewed passages, with full raw PDFs preserved; neither claims full OCR. Other retained derivatives are identified by extraction method and hash. Constitution, legislative history, publisher guidance and update tables must retain their distinct source types rather than being labelled enacted statutes.
