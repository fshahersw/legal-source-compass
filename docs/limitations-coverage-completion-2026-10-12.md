# Time Limits coverage release, 2026-10-12

The `2026-10-12.4` release retains the `2026-10-12.3` addition of 21 source-bound rules for Arkansas, Georgia, Mississippi and Tennessee: 13 explicitly selected narrow variants that can produce a baseline after review, and eight flagged research entries that cannot issue dates. It incorporates the separately reviewed `2026-10-12.2` counting candidate and adds the current Georgia statutory counting provision. The release has been staged and hash-verified privately, and activated in the local server manifest; production deployment and full live bundle verification have now completed.

The prior 889 rules, their grades and currency, and all prior source text remain unchanged. The combined candidates add 28 retained sources: ten for counting and 18 for claims. General coverage changes from 539 baseline labels, 59 flagged, 13 missing and one research-only to 528 baseline labels, 77 flagged, six missing and one research-only. Eleven additional flags correct earlier misleading coverage labels on rules whose assessed dates were already withheld; seven additional flags represent newly documented general claim categories.

## What becomes available

- Sales-warranty variants distinguish Mississippi's six-year period from the four-year Arkansas, Georgia and Tennessee periods. They require the ordinary goods-delivery scope and exclude unresolved future-performance, shorter contractual terms and prior-action issues. The different saving provisions remain visible and are not automatically applied.
- Arkansas has separate one-year spoken-slander and three-year written-libel variants. Tennessee has six-month spoken slander and a one-year libel variant excluding a related criminal prosecution. These do not become one general defamation clock.
- Named assault/battery/false-imprisonment variants cover only the causes expressly supported by the Arkansas and Mississippi provisions. Tennessee's separate false-imprisonment variant excludes a related criminal prosecution; the conditional two-year provision remains recorded.
- Arkansas attorney-negligence uses a confirmed occurrence date only after concealment review, including the substituted *Nichols* opinion's treatment of silence. Mississippi's negligence variant uses independently confirmed discovery under *Bradley*. Georgia malpractice remains flagged: current statutory text and *Coe* establish useful research, but not a resolved general accrual rule or every contract theory.
- Georgia statutory weekend counting uses current O.C.G.A. §1-3-1(d)(3), subject to a law’s specific computation rule. Only the ordinary statutory weekend endpoint is adjusted; holidays, periods shorter than seven days, hours, contracts and independent outer bars require separate review.
- New York statutory weekend counting is supported by current official GCL25-a and the official *Chen* application. Holidays, contractual periods and special bars are not inferred. Refreshed CT, DC, MT, NH and TN evidence retains its existing counting flags.

## Remaining limits

The six general gaps are Arkansas and Tennessee defamation and the four states' broad intentional-tort categories. They have different periods or unresolved mappings; supported narrow variants are listed separately. The other pre-existing flagged rules and Iowa product-liability research remain unresolved. Counting has 33 verified rows and 18 flagged rows, with no absent state row. A recorded row still does not establish a complete holiday, service or filing-cutoff calendar. This work does not claim complete legal coverage or comprehensive current-law/citator review.
